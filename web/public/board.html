Where the figures come from

Once a day the board reads each platform's own public list. It needs no account anywhere, and it shows a program only while the platform lists it as open to the public.

  • HackerOne. HackerOne’s public program directory, with scope from the open bounty-targets-data set.
  • Bugcrowd. The open bounty-targets-data set, read from Bugcrowd’s public engagements.
  • Intigriti. The open bounty-targets-data set, read from Intigriti’s public programs.
  • YesWeHack. The open bounty-targets-data set, read from YesWeHack’s public programs.
  • Immunefi. Immunefi’s public bounty list.
  • Cantina. Cantina’s public opportunities list.
  • Sherlock. Sherlock’s public bug bounty and contest lists.
  • Code4rena. Code4rena’s public audit list.

Every figure is the platform's own. A reward is shown in the currency the platform publishes it in, and ordering by reward compares the figures as published, without converting. Where a platform does not publish something, the cell is empty; nothing is estimated.

New. A launch date is the platform's where it publishes one. Where it does not, the board uses the day it first saw the program, and says "first listed". An asset counts as added to scope on the day the board first saw it, or on the platform's own date where it gives one. The history of the four web2 platforms comes from the commit log of the open bounty-targets-data set (MIT licence): one day a month back to December 2025, one a week from August 2026 and every day from 20 October 2026, so a first-listed date before then is the end of its week or month.

Cost to file. Immunefi marks a program "Pay to Submit" and shows the amount when you submit. Cantina publishes a submission fee per bounty. Sherlock takes a stake with a report and returns it when the report is valid. HackerOne's response time is the latest figure in its own directory and can differ from the one on the program page.

The program page is the authority. Read it before you rely on anything here.
